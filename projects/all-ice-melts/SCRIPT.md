# SCRIPT — all-ice-melts (what-if ep. 3, 3D cartoon)

## Sources
- ~70 m of sea-level rise if all glaciers and ice sheets melted (USGS FAQ "How would sea level change if all glaciers melted?"); USGS Fact Sheet 117-95 gives ~80 m. 70 m ≈ a 20-storey building (3-3.5 m per storey).
- Antarctica holds ~91 % of the world's ice, Greenland ~8 %, mountain glaciers ~1 % (USGS Fact Sheet 117-95 "Sea level change: lessons from the geologic record").
- Melting floating sea ice barely changes sea level (it already displaces its own weight: Archimedes); land ice is what raises it (NASA/FAU "sea ice" teaching pages; mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov "When floating ice melts"; Skeptical Science). A tiny salinity effect exists but is negligible.
- A complete melt would take many thousands of years (National Geographic "Rising Seas" feature, cited secondhand; educational sources say "many thousands of years").
- Sea level is already rising, ~3.3 mm per year (NASA sea level data; cited in the search results).
