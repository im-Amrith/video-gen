# SCRIPT: What If You Jumped Into a Hole Through the Earth?

## Sources (checked with web search, 2026-11-04)
- Fall time 38 min 11 s (real-Earth PREM model), 42 min 12 s (uniform Earth), 38 min 0 s (constant gravity): A. Klotz, "The gravity tunnel in a non-uniform Earth", *Am. J. Phys.* 83, 231 (2015), arXiv:1308.1342; phys.org (2015-03) "New way to calculate how long it would take to fall through a hole in the Earth"; Live Science 50312.
- Gravity peaks near ~10.7 m/s² around 3,000 km deep, then falls to zero at the centre (shell theorem): oikofuge.com "Falling Through The Earth"; gauravtiwari.org "Tunnel through the Earth".
- Peak speed in a vacuum tunnel: ~7.9 km/s ≈ 28,400 km/h for a uniform Earth; higher for the real Earth (our own integration of a PREM-like gravity profile gives ~10 km/s, 38.0 min one way, 12.7 min to the core). Hence "over 28,000 km/h". ScienceABC "What would happen if you dug a tunnel…"; physicsforums "speed of falling object at Earth's centre".
- Layers: mantle to ~2,900 km; liquid iron-nickel outer core to ~5,150 km; solid inner core to the centre at 6,371 km: Britannica "Earth's layers"; worldatlas "layers of the Earth".
- ~6,000 ± 500 °C at the inner-core boundary: Anzellini et al., *Science* 340, 464 (2013); ESRF news "Earth Center Hotter" (2013). Sun's visible surface ≈ 5,500 °C (NASA Sun facts).
- Deepest hole: Kola Superdeep Borehole, 12,262 m (~180 °C at the bottom); 12.26 km ÷ 12,742 km ≈ 0.1 % of Earth's diameter: Wikipedia "Kola Superdeep Borehole"; Guinness World Records.
- With air in the tunnel, drag caps the fall at ~200 km/h within ~10 s; you would come to rest at the centre: C. Baird, West Texas A&M "Science Questions with Surprising Answers" (2013-10-04). 6,371 km ÷ 200 km/h ≈ 32 h, hence "more than a day".
- Pole-to-pole so Earth's spin (Coriolis) doesn't push you into the wall: standard physics, noted in Klotz (2015).
