# SCRIPT — What If You Jumped Into a Hole Through the Earth? (What If ep. 1, 3D cartoon version)

## Sources (checked with web search, 2026-11-04)
- Fall time 38 min 11 s (real-Earth PREM model), 42 min 12 s (uniform Earth), 38 min 0 s (constant gravity): A. Klotz, *Am. J. Phys.* 83, 231 (2015), arXiv:1308.1342; phys.org (2015-03).
- Gravity *increases* with depth to ~10.7 m/s² near the core-mantle boundary (~2,900 km) because the dense iron core is closer, then falls to zero at the centre: oikofuge.com "Falling Through The Earth"; Klotz (2015).
- ~13 min to reach the core (our PREM-like integration: 12.7 min; 38.0 min one way).
- Outer core = liquid iron-nickel from ~2,900 to ~5,150 km: Britannica "Earth's layers". Inner-core boundary ~6,000 ± 500 °C (≈ Sun's visible surface, ~5,500 °C): Anzellini et al., *Science* 340, 464 (2013); ESRF press release; NASA Sun facts.
- Peak speed in a vacuum tunnel ≈ 7.9 km/s ≈ 28,400 km/h for a uniform Earth, higher for the real Earth → "over 28,000 km/h": ScienceABC; physicsforums.
- Kola Superdeep Borehole: 12,262 m; ~180 °C at the bottom, drilling stopped because of the heat: Wikipedia; Guinness World Records; WorldAtlas.
- Falling 12.3 km at ~9.8 m/s² takes ~50 s → "within a minute".
- With air: drag caps the fall near 200 km/h within ~10 s and you come to rest at the centre (C. Baird, West Texas A&M, 2013); 6,371 km ÷ 200 km/h ≈ 32 h → "more than a day".
- Pole-to-pole so Earth's spin doesn't push you into the wall (Coriolis): Klotz (2015).
- The motion is simple harmonic, like a swing/pendulum: fastest in the middle, momentarily stopped at each end.
