# The Tower That Started Falling Before It Was Finished

**Title:** Why the Leaning Tower of Pisa Leans 🗼

**Description:** The Leaning Tower of Pisa started tipping when it was only two or three floors tall. A century-long pause, a banana-shaped fix, 900 tonnes of lead and some very careful drilling later — it's still leaning, on purpose.

**Hashtags:** #history #pisa #engineering #italy #shorts

**Sources**
- Construction began Aug 1173; ~3 m-deep foundation on soft clay, sand and silt; sinking began around 1178 after the second (or third) floor: Wikipedia "Leaning Tower of Pisa"; WorldAtlas "Why does the Leaning Tower of Pisa lean?".
- Work halted for almost a century (wars with Genoa, Lucca, Florence); the pause let the soil consolidate, probably saving the tower: Wikipedia.
- From 1272 the upper floors were built taller on the short side, giving the curved ("banana") shape; the bell chamber was completed in 1372: Wikipedia.
- Closed in 1990 at a lean of about 5.5°; reopened Dec 2001: ICE "Stabilising the Leaning Tower of Pisa"; Designing Buildings Wiki.
- Lead counterweights on the north (high) side from 1993 (~600 t, later ~900 t); soil under-excavation with inclined drills 2000-2001 (~38 m³ removed): PBS NOVA "Pisa: interventions"; Practical Engineering (2023); Ingenia (J. Burland).
- Result: about 40 cm (38-43 cm) less lean at the top, just under 4°: ICE; The Local (2018); ScienceAlert.
- Note: the lean in the film is drawn ×1.5 for readability.
