# What If All the Ice on Earth Melted?

**Hook:** "There's enough ice on land to bury a 20-storey building under the sea, everywhere on every coast."

**Story in 5 beats:** hook (70 m ≈ a 20-storey building) → setting (where the ice is: Antarctica 91 %, Greenland 8 %, glaciers 1 %) →
escalation (water rises floor by floor up a coastal city) → payoff (coastlines redrawn; whole low countries gone) →
twist (melting sea ice adds almost nothing, it already floats: it's the land ice that matters; and it would take thousands of years).

**Key facts + sources:**
- ~70 m (USGS FAQ "How would sea level change if all glaciers melted?"); ~80 m (USGS Fact Sheet 117-95 "Sea level change: lessons from the geologic record"). Say "about 70 metres".
- Antarctica 91 %, Greenland 8 %, mountain glaciers ~1 % of the world's ice. USGS FS 117-95.
- Floating sea ice doesn't raise sea level when it melts (Archimedes). NSIDC.
- Timescale: thousands of years (National Geographic 2013 "Rising Seas" map feature; verify before the script).

**Visual ideas:** an iso coastal city board with a water column rising floor by floor (strata cross-section), an Isotype ice tally
(91 / 8 / 1 cubes), a glass of water with an ice cube for the twist.
