# What If You Jumped Into a Hole Through the Earth?

**Hook:** "If you jumped into a hole straight through the Earth, you'd come out the other side in thirty-eight minutes. Then it gets weird."

**Story in 5 beats:** hook (a person at the lip of a pole-to-pole hole) → setting (the deepest hole we ever dug: 12 km, 0.2 % of the way) →
escalation (falling through crust, mantle, outer core, past 28,000 km/h, ~6,000 °C) → payoff (weightless at the centre, you
coast up and stop exactly at the far side after ~38 min) → twist (with air in the tunnel, drag wins: you'd end up floating at the centre, forever).

**Key facts + sources:**
- 38 min 11 s with the real (PREM) Earth; 42 min 12 s if Earth were uniform; 38 min 0 s with constant gravity. Klotz, *Am. J. Phys.* 83, 231 (2015), arXiv:1308.1342; phys.org 2015-03 "fall-hole-earth".
- Peak speed at the centre: ~7.9 km/s ≈ 28,400 km/h for the uniform model, higher for the real Earth (say "over 28,000 km/h"). ScienceABC; physicsforums.
- Gravity falls to zero at the centre (shell theorem). Gravity actually *rises* to ~10.7 m/s² near 3,000 km depth (core-mantle boundary) before falling.
- Layers: mantle to ~2,900 km, outer core (liquid iron-nickel) to ~5,150 km, inner core to the centre at 6,371 km. Britannica "Earth's layers".
- Inner-core boundary ~6,000 ± 500 °C. Anzellini et al., *Science* 340, 464 (2013); ESRF press release.
- Kola Superdeep Borehole: 12,262 m, ~180 °C at the bottom. Wikipedia "Kola Superdeep Borehole"; Guinness.
- Tunnel must run pole to pole, or Earth's spin pushes you into the wall (Coriolis).
- With air drag, each swing is shorter and you settle at the centre (damped oscillation).

speed and temperature gauges as label pins; the Kola hole as a hair-thin line next to the tunnel (scale reveal); a ping-pong path on the return.
