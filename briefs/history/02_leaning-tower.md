# The Tower That Started Falling Before It Was Finished

**Hook (first line of the voice-over):** "The Leaning Tower of Pisa started tipping over when it was only two floors tall. So the builders... went to war for a hundred years."

**Story in 5 beats:** hook (a two-storey stub of white marble, already tilted) → setting (Pisa, 1173: a bell tower on soft clay, sand and silt, with a foundation only ~3 m deep; by about 1178 it's sinking) → escalation (wars with Genoa, Lucca and Florence stop work for nearly a century; the pause lets the soil settle; in 1272 builders return and make the new floors taller on the short side, so the tower bends like a banana; the bell chamber goes on in 1372) → snap (by 1990 it leans about 5.5° and is closed; engineers stack ~600-900 t of lead on the high side, then gently drill out ~38 m³ of soil from under it, 2000-2001) → deadpan + twist ("Luckily, they pulled it back about 40 centimetres. Unluckily, it's still leaning. On purpose, this time.")

**Key facts + sources:**
- Construction began Aug 1173; shallow ~3 m foundation on soft clay/sand/silt; sinking began around 1178 after the second floor (sources say second or third). Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaning_Tower_of_Pisa ; WorldAtlas, https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/why-does-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa-lean.html
- Work halted for almost a century (wars with Genoa, Lucca, Florence); the pause let the soil consolidate, probably saving it. Wikipedia (above)
- From 1272 the upper floors were built taller on one side to compensate, giving the curved shape; completed with the bell chamber in 1372. Wikipedia (above)
- Closed in 1990, reopened Dec 2001; lean had reached about 5.5°. ICE, https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/what-do-civil-engineers-do/stabilising-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa/ ; Designing Buildings, https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Stabilising_the_Leaning_Tower_of_Pisa
- Lead counterweights on the north side from 1993 (about 600 t, later ~900 t); soil underexcavation via inclined drills Feb 2000 - Jun 2001, ~38 m³ (~70 t) removed. PBS NOVA, https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/pisa/interventions2.html ; Practical Engineering, https://practical.engineering/blog/2023/12/19/how-engineers-straightened-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa ; Ingenia (Burland), https://ingenia.org.uk/articles/the-stabilisation-of-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa/
- Result: about half a degree less lean, ~38-43 cm at the top (to just under 4°); a further ~4 cm by 2018. ICE (above); The Local, https://www.thelocal.it/20181122/the-leaning-tower-of-pisa-is-losing-its-lean ; ScienceAlert, https://www.sciencealert.com/we-can-all-relax-now-pisa-s-leaning-tower-isn-t-quite-so-leaning

- Frame 0: a medieval builder on a ladder pressing a spirit level to a two-storey stub; the bubble slides off the end. *Plink.*

- Callback: tourists doing the "holding it up" pose; the narrator notes they're helping.

**Notes:** Pure engineering comedy, no casualties involved. Say "about 40 centimetres" (sources give 38-43 cm). Direction detail: north side got the lead; avoid claiming which way it first leaned (sources conflict). Not verified, leave out: Galileo's dropping-balls experiment from the tower (likely legend), and how many centuries the fix will last (sources disagree; don't give a number).
