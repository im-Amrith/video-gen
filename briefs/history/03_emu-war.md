# The Army That Lost to Birds

**Hook (first line of the voice-over):** "In 1932, Australia sent soldiers with machine guns to fight a war against emus. The emus won."

**Story in 5 beats:** hook (a soldier with a machine gun facing a horizon of feathery heads) → setting (Western Australia, 1932: veterans farming wheat near Campion; drought sends ~20,000 emus into the fields, flattening crops and fences) → escalation (2 Nov 1932: Major G.P.W. Meredith arrives with two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds; a planned ambush at a dam ends when the gun jams; a gun bolted on a truck can't keep up across rough ground with birds running ~50 km/h) → snap (second try, 13 Nov - 10 Dec: by Meredith's own count, 9,860 rounds for 986 birds, about ten bullets per emu; the army is recalled) → deadpan + twist ("Luckily, the army had brought ten thousand bullets. Unluckily, the emus had brought twenty thousand emus.") Callback: the farmers go back to bounties, and the emus never sign a peace treaty.

**Key facts + sources:**
- Drought-driven emu migration damaged wheat in Western Australia, especially the Campion district; many farmers were WWI veterans. Britannica, https://www.britannica.com/topic/Emu-War ; Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emu_War
- About 20,000 emus. Australian Geographic, https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/topics/wildlife/2016/10/australias-emu-wars/ ; History Hit, https://www.historyhit.com/the-great-emu-war/
- Major Gwynydd Purves Wynne-Aubrey Meredith, Royal Australian Artillery, with two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds; operation from 2 Nov to 10 Dec 1932 in two phases. Wikipedia (above); Mental Floss, https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/84904/when-australia-went-war-its-emus
- An early ambush: the gun jammed after about a dozen birds were hit and the flock escaped; a truck-mounted gun couldn't keep up on rough ground. Wikipedia (above); MeatEater, https://themeateater.com/conservation/wildlife-management/the-great-emu-war-of-1932
- Meredith reported 986 killed for 9,860 rounds (his own claim, not an independent count). Wikipedia (above); Australian Geographic (above)
- Emus can sprint at about 48-50 km/h. Victoria Wildlife fact sheet, https://www.wildlife.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/word_doc/0026/91385/Emu.docx
- Afterwards a bounty system for farmers was used instead and proved more effective. Wikipedia (above)

**Visual ideas:**
- Golden wheat sea under a hot blue sky; heads of emus pop up one by one, then hundreds, like popcorn.
- Three bean-head soldiers in slouch hats with a big comic Lewis gun on a tripod, sweat drops flying.
- The jam: the gun goes "click... click"; the emus look at each other and sprint off in a dust cloud.
- The truck chase: a wobbling truck bouncing over dunes, gun spraying sky, emus casually jogging ahead and looking back.
- A scoreboard on a fence post: "BULLETS 9,860 / EMUS 986"; the narrator divides it out loud.
- Callback: an emu wearing the major's hat, standing on the hill like a general.

**Notes:** Light, deadpan, but no shots of birds being hit; cut away to dust, feathers floating, jams and misses. Say "by the army's own count" for 986. Unverified, left out: Meredith's famous "bullet-carrying capacity... invulnerability of tanks" quote (I could not confirm the wording); the claim that emus "posted lookouts" or used "guerrilla tactics" (anthropomorphic retelling). Keep the 20,000 figure as "about twenty thousand".
