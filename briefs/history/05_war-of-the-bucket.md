# The War of the Bucket

**Hook (first line of the voice-over):** "In 1325, two Italian cities fought a battle with thousands of soldiers, and the winners went home with... a bucket."

**Story in 5 beats:** hook (a wooden bucket glowing on a velvet cushion, a trophy spotlight) → setting (Modena and Bologna, neighbours 40 km apart and old rivals: Modena with the emperor's side, Bologna with the pope's; Modena grabs Bologna's castle of Monteveglio and Bologna declares war) → escalation (15 Nov 1325, Zappolino: a big Bolognese army, said to be ~32,000, meets ~7,000 Modenese under Passerino Bonacolsi; a fake river crossing pulls part of Bologna away; the Bolognese break and run home before sunset) → snap (Modena chases them right to the walls of Bologna, and for a trophy takes a wooden bucket from the well by Porta San Felice) → deadpan + twist ("Luckily, everyone remembers the war. Unluckily, they remember it as the war that started over a bucket, when the bucket came at the end.") Callback: the bucket is still in Modena's town hall, and a copy hangs in the Ghirlandina tower.

**Key facts + sources:**
- Battle of Zappolino, 15 Nov 1325, Modena (Ghibelline) beat Bologna (Guelph); part of the long Guelph-Ghibelline struggle. Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Zappolino ; Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Bucket
- Real trigger: Modena took the Bolognese castle of Monteveglio; the "stolen bucket started it" story is a myth. Wikipedia "War of the Bucket" (above); The Collector, https://www.thecollector.com/medieval-legend-war-bucket/
- Armies: ~32,000 Bolognese vs ~7,000 Modenese (Wikipedia; HistoryNet says 5,000 foot + 2,000 horse for Modena); Bonacolsi used a feigned river crossing. Wikipedia (above); HistoryNet, https://historynet.com/what-we-learned-from-the-battle-of-zappolino-1325/
- The Modenese pursued to Bologna's walls and took a bucket from a well outside Porta San Felice as a trophy. Wikipedia "Battle of Zappolino" (above); Bologna Welcome, https://www.bolognawelcome.com/en/places/towers-historic-buildings/porta-san-felice-2
- The bucket was kept for ~650 years in the Ghirlandina tower; the original is now in the Camerino dei Confirmati in Modena's Palazzo Comunale, with a copy in the tower. Comune di Modena, https://archiviopatrimoniomondiale.comune.modena.it/en/plan-your-visit/municipal-headquarters ; Visit Modena, https://www.visitmodena.it/en/palazzo-comunale-inglese.pdf/@@download/file
- Made famous by Alessandro Tassoni's mock-heroic poem *La secchia rapita* (published 1622). Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_secchia_rapita

**Visual ideas:**
- Frame 0: an ordinary wooden bucket on a red cushion under a museum spotlight, a velvet rope in front, a guard standing to attention.
- Two walled toy-like cities on green hills with banners, facing each other across a valley; a little castle (Monteveglio) changes flag with a *pop*.
- The fake crossing: Modenese soldiers splash at a river ford while the Bolognese all turn to look; the rest of Modena walks around behind them.
- The chase at sunset: Bolognese bean-heads running for the city gate, holding their helmets on.
- The theft: one Modenese soldier at a stone well by a city gate, untying the bucket with great ceremony, carrying it home on his shoulder like a crown.
- Callback: today's tourists in Modena's town hall staring at the bucket; the narrator: "Bologna still hasn't got it back."

**Notes:** Light and deadpan. Roughly 2,000 died: say "a lot of people got hurt" once at most, no battle gore; show flags, dust and running. Correction to the brief: the bucket did not cause the war; it was taken after the battle. Make that the twist. Numbers vary by source, so say "about thirty thousand" vs "about seven thousand". Dropped: any claim that Bologna has formally asked for the bucket back or that the cities "are still technically at war" (not verified); the "Bologna still hasn't got it back" line is true because the bucket is still in Modena.
